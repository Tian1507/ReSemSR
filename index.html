ReSemSR: A Speech Super-Resolution Model with Reconstruction-Aware Semantic Guidance

Yuan Tian, Hui-Peng Du,

Abstract

This paper proposes ReSemSR, a Reconstruction-aware Semantic-guided framework for speech Super-Resolution, targeting the increased ambiguity of recovering missing spectral components under severe bandwidth limitation. ReSemSR first performs coarse acoustic reconstruction and then exploits the resulting intermediate acoustic representation together with the low-resolution spectrum to predict the semantic-unit posterior of the target speech through a compact reconstruction-aware semantic prediction module. During training, a frozen HuBERT teacher model and a fixed K-means codebook provide semantic supervision, while HuBERT is completely removed during inference. The predicted semantic-unit posterior is then mapped to a continuous semantic representation and incorporated into the acoustic refinement network to guide the reconstruction of missing high-frequency components. Experimental results on 16 kHz speech super-resolution demonstrate that ReSemSR achieves competitive spectral and perceptual quality while providing increasingly pronounced intelligibility improvements as the input bandwidth decreases, and requires substantially lower computational complexity than the compared semantic-guided method.
